09 · Apex

Round 3 All directions

Serious, but it should feel like you are winning. Graphite surfaces and adult restraint on the outside; heavy italic display type, thick rings and an obvious percentage on every screen on the inside. A personal best is the loudest thing the app ever says. Progress is gamified the way Whoop, Strava and Apple's rings do it: levels and XP, a streak you can lose, tiered metal medals with progress to the next tier. No stickers, no confetti, no slang.

Palette

#0A0B0D bg #16191D tile #262B31 hairline #C3F04A lime #F2B53C amber #8A97B2 steel

Lime = act, progress, PB. Amber is the one secondary and appears only on streaks and gold medals. Silver and bronze tiers are steel and a darker amber. Glow is spent on PB and level-up moments only.

Type

Geist 800–900, uppercase, set big at -0.055em and skewed 8° on hero numbers and headlines for forward motion. Body is Geist 400–600. Geist Mono appears only for tiny units and the receipt. Fallbacks: Inter, SF Pro, system-ui.

Signature move

Every screen answers "how close am I" with a thick lime bar or ring and a percentage, and one number is huge. PBs get the full-width, glowing "NEW PB 12 → 14" treatment; levels and medals give each session something to fill.

Trade-offs

Wins

Progress is never ambiguous. Each screen carries a thick bar or ring with a percentage and an "X to go" line: level, weekly goal, streak, quest, session, PB target, badges, body goal. A PB gets the largest type in the whole app.

Costs

  • More to look at. Today now stacks level, hero, goal, streak, quest, lineup and week. It scrolls about two screens, and the XP and quest layers are new product scope: XP rules, level curve, quest generation and badge tiers all need defining and tuning before they are honest numbers.
  • Gamification can nag. "Don't break it" and "PB in reach" are motivating but pushy. Each should be switchable (Settings: celebrate / plain), and streak warnings should only appear when the week is actually at risk.
  • Loud type needs discipline. Heavy italics on every number would turn into noise. Here it is limited to one hero figure per tile and uppercase headlines; supporting text stays in regular weight. Skewed type also looks slightly different from true italics, so test it with the real font.
  • Two accents. Amber is held to streaks and gold medals. If it spreads to PBs or buttons, the lime stops meaning "act".

Shipping on iOS 15 WebKit (Obsidian mobile)

  • Glow is box-shadow, text-shadow and drop-shadow, spent only on PB, level-up and medal moments. Several stacked drop-shadows on animating SVG are the main cost, so keep the halo animation to one element at a time on older devices. backdrop-filter is on the nav only (prefixed, solid fallback via @supports not).
  • Hex medals use clip-path: polygon() (iOS 15 fine) with a nested inset clip for the bevel. A drop-shadow filter on a clipped element is applied after the clip, so the glow follows the hexagon.
  • Skewed type is transform: skewX(-8deg) on inline-blocks, not font-style: italic, so it works with any weight. Allow a little right padding so glyphs are not clipped inside overflow: hidden tiles.
  • Fonts. Geist 400/600/800/900 (and a small Geist Mono subset for units) would need bundling as woff2 via font.css because Obsidian mobile is often offline; the fallback stack lands on SF Pro, where 900 renders as Heavy/Black and keeps the idea. Rings are SVG with pathLength; avoided: :has(), color-mix(), dvh, @property. aspect-ratio has a padding fallback.
  • Share card is rendered to an image (1080 × 1920); the receipt here is the on-screen preview only.
