Trade-offs

Wins

Densest of the five — the whole session card, the week, four stats and two goals fit above one scroll on desktop, and tabular mono numerals make "7 vs 8 reps" comparable at a glance in a way proportional figures never are. Real <table> markup is also the most screen-reader-legible of the set.

Costs

It reads as an instrument, not a coach — no emotional lift on a day you don't feel like training, which is exactly when a training app earns its keep. Tables are also the most fragile layout at 390px: any new column forces a horizontal scroll or a stacked-row fallback, and long exercise names crowd the numeric columns.

Mapping onto src/styles.css

  • Add --gv-mono and apply it to .gv-tile-big, .gv-goal-nums, .gv-set-input, .gv-log-clock, plus font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums — that alone gets ~60% of this direction with no markup change.
  • .gv-set-table / .gv-log-sets stop being flex rows and become a real <table>; that's the one structural change, and it touches page-log.js and page-history.js, not just CSS.
  • --gv-radius → 0, --gv-shadow → none, and --gv-line does all separation as full-width rules. Keep --gv-lime for fills only (bars, ticks, the today cell) and --gv-lime-deep for every numeral that carries meaning — 5.0:1 on white.
