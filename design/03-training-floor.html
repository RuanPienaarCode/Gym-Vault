The Library

Straight-bar Dips

  1. Support yourself on top of a single straight bar, hands just outside your hips, arms locked.
  2. Lean slightly forward and bend the elbows until the bar reaches your lower chest / upper abs.
  3. Press back to a full lockout at the top.

Cue: the other half of the muscle-up — the top-out is exactly this position.

Trade-offs

Wins

Highest motivational charge of the five — the hero is unmissable, the chunky bars make a 46% plank feel like progress rather than a statistic, and the 48px tick targets are the easiest to hit with sweaty hands mid-set. It's the only direction that actively pushes you.

Costs

Loud gets tiring: microcopy that lands on day 3 grates on day 300, and 3px borders plus clip-path cuts are heavy visual furniture inside an Obsidian pane that already has its own chrome. Diagonal clips also complicate responsive edges — the cut angle has to be re-tuned at desktop width or it reads as a mistake.

Mapping onto src/styles.css

  • .gv-hero keeps its lime gradient but drops border-radius for a clip-path cut and goes full-bleed — which means .gv-page's padding: 4px 18px has to move onto the inner sections instead of the scroll container.
  • .gv-bar grows 8px → 18px with a 3px solid var(--gv-ink) frame and a repeating-gradient stripe on .gv-bar-fill; same markup, same width calculation from goalProgress().
  • Borders go from 1px solid var(--gv-line) to 3px solid var(--gv-ink) across .gv-card, .gv-tile, .gv-log-set, and --gv-radius drops to 0. Watch the light-mode button trap noted at the top of the file — every new .gv-btn colour rule still needs the .gv-app ancestor to beat app.css.
